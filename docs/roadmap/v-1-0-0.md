# v1.0.0 — Production-ready

- **Version**: `v1.0.0`
- **Status**: planned
- **Theme**: Production-ready: safe, legal, reproducible, and adopted by someone other than its author
- **Released**: —

> **In one sentence**: redstep is trustworthy enough for real use by someone who did not build it — proven by a real adoption example — with the governance, supply-chain, and disclosure maturity a 1.0 promises, not merely more features.

## Goal

1.0 is a trust claim, not a feature claim. Every release up to here proves a capability; 1.0 proves the project: that the whole thing is safe, legal, and reproducible enough for someone other than its author to adopt. The deciding evidence is a real adoption example — redstep run by someone else, against a real application or environment, finding real value with no scope or lab escape. Around that sit the maturity the number promises: the risk-tier governance and the legal review complete enough to distribute worldwide, the attack-data packs and attacker image under sustained auditable maintenance, a responsible-disclosure process, and the reliability a long-running tool needs. It is deliberately a high and distant bar; the point of the long 0.x series is to earn it rather than declare it.

## Scope

- A real-world adoption example: redstep used by someone other than the author, against a real target, with demonstrated value and no safety incident — the evidence that 1.0 is earned rather than asserted.
- Governance and legal maturity: the risk-tier distribution model and the US/Japan legal review complete enough to distribute worldwide — realizes `ADR-0021`.
- Supply-chain maturity: the attack-data packs and the attacker image profiles under sustained, auditable maintenance, with a current SBOM and license review — realizes `ADR-0018` / `ADR-0019`.
- A responsible-disclosure process for vulnerabilities redstep's own use surfaces in third-party software.
- Reliability hardening: the long-running-tool robustness surfaces closed (for example the Docker-await surface so a probe cannot hang, #42), and the suite green across supported platforms.
- Coverage as a decision: every in-scope catalog technique is either built or explicitly marked out of scope, so the remaining `capability: none` entries are a conscious boundary, not an accidental gap.

## Attack coverage

None new. This release completes or consciously bounds the existing catalog rather than adding a technique; the coverage criterion below is about closing the backlog as a decision, not shipping a new check.

## Out of scope

- New capability themes beyond the 0.x series: anything genuinely new is a post-1.0 release (1.x and beyond), not a reason to delay 1.0.
- Any real-backend reach that has not been through the risk-tier governance: 1.0 does not relax the isolation or scope guarantees, it audits them.

## Depends on

Every prior release across the 0.x series (v0.1.0 onward): 1.0 is the point where the capability set they built is audited as a whole for the trust claim, not a place new capability lands. It is reached when its criteria are met, not immediately after the last themed release; broadening and hardening releases sit between that release and this gate.

## Release criteria

- [ ] A real-world adoption example exists: redstep used by someone other than the author against a real target, with demonstrated value and no scope or lab escape, documented.
- [ ] The risk-tier governance and the US/Japan legal review are complete enough to distribute worldwide (ADR-0021).
- [ ] The attack-data packs and attacker image are under auditable maintenance with a current SBOM and license review (ADR-0018, ADR-0019).
- [ ] A responsible-disclosure process is published and reachable.
- [ ] Reliability: the known hang and robustness surfaces are closed (#42), and the suite is green across every supported platform.
- [ ] Every in-scope catalog technique is either built or explicitly marked out of scope; the coverage view shows a conscious boundary, not an accidental gap.
- [ ] The off-scale guarantee (no action escapes its scope or the lab, no egress to real infrastructure) is audited end to end across every real-backend feature added since v0.8.0.
- [ ] The workspace version is bumped to 1.0.0 and the release is cut.

## Work

## Risks and open questions

- The central risk is declaring 1.0 before it is earned: calling it production-ready without a real adoption example and mature governance would make the version number a claim the project cannot back. The adoption example is the guard against a premature 1.0.
- 1.0 is where the off-scale guarantee is audited across the real-backend features (real cloud in v0.8.0, real Windows/AD in v0.9.0, the autonomous operator and C2 in v0.10.0). A gap there is not a wrong answer, it is real-world harm; the audit is end to end, not per feature.
- Open: whether 1.0 requires the full in-scope catalog built, or a defined core built with the rest consciously out of scope — leaning to the latter (coverage as a decision), to settle before the release.
