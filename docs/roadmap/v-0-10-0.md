# v0.10.0 — AI-driven testing and adversary emulation

- **Version**: `v0.10.0`
- **Status**: planned
- **Theme**: AI-driven testing and adversary emulation
- **Released**: —

> **In one sentence**: an AI operator explores for attacks nobody scripted and promotes what it finds into reproducible pinned tests, and a command-and-control session layer drives multi-step adversary emulation — both inside the same scope, intensity, and egress-less guarantees as every other capability.

## Goal

This release is the two ends that make redstep more than a fixed suite of checks. The AI operator runs in two modes — free exploration to find attacks nobody wrote down, and curated verification — and the value is the promotion step: a discovery becomes a pinned, reproducible test, so an AI finding is a regression the suite keeps, not a one-off transcript. The command-and-control session layer is the foothold and implant lifecycle on top of the v0.5.0 pivot, for multi-step adversary emulation that resembles a real intrusion campaign. Both are high-autonomy and high-intensity, so both are the sharpest test of the safety model the whole project rests on: an AI driving attacks and a C2 implant must stay inside scope, inside the intensity gate, and inside the egress-less lab exactly like a hand-written capability. The AI proposes; the gates dispose.

## Scope

- The AI operator abstraction: an Exploration mode (free, to find unknown attacks) and a Verification mode (curated), behind a provider abstraction (Claude, OpenAI) so the operator is not tied to one model — realizes `ADR-0015`.
- Promotion of a discovery to a pinned, reproducible test: an attack the operator finds is captured as a Success Witness and generated into a replay, so exploration output becomes a regression, not a transcript — realizes `ADR-0015` / `ADR-0022`.
- The command-and-control session layer: the foothold and implant lifecycle on top of the multi-step pivot, for multi-step adversary emulation (Sliver, Mythic) — realizes `ADR-0020`.
- Testing AI applications: the LLM-application techniques against an AI target — prompt injection, improper output handling, system prompt leakage, sensitive-information disclosure, and excessive agency — realizes the AI genre of the catalog.
- The safety model extended to autonomy: the AI operator and the C2 implant run only inside the scope gate, the intensity ladder, and the egress-less lab; nothing the operator proposes bypasses a gate, and C2 sits at a high distribution risk tier — realizes `ADR-0006` / `ADR-0011` / `ADR-0021`.

## Out of scope

- Production-readiness, governance, and the real-world adoption example — deferred to `v1.0.0`.
- New non-AI attack techniques: this release adds the AI genre and the operator/C2 machinery, not more Web/network/cloud techniques.

## Depends on

v0.5.0 (the multi-step pivot the C2 session layer extends), v0.1.0 (the Success Witness and pinned replay the promotion step uses, and the scope/intensity gates the operator runs inside), v0.7.0 (the scenario form a promoted discovery is captured as), and v0.2.0-v0.9.0 (the capability set the operator explores with).

## Release criteria

- [ ] The AI operator runs an exploration against a lab target and a verification pass, behind a provider abstraction, inside the scope and intensity gates (a test proves it cannot act outside scope or above its authorized intensity).
- [ ] A discovery is promoted to a pinned, reproducible test that replays deterministically, so an AI finding becomes a regression (ADR-0015, ADR-0022).
- [ ] The C2 session layer establishes a foothold and runs a multi-step emulation on the pivot, confined to the egress-less lab and gated at a high risk tier.
- [ ] The LLM-application techniques run against an AI target with evidence and a verdict; their catalog capabilities are set.
- [ ] Neither the AI operator nor the C2 implant can escape scope, intensity, or the lab; the boundary self-check and the gates hold against an autonomous driver, which is audited explicitly.
- [ ] The workspace version is bumped to 0.10.0 and the release is cut.

## Risks and open questions

- Autonomy is the project's hardest safety case: an AI that chooses actions, and a C2 implant that runs them, are exactly where "an action escapes its scope or the lab" could happen without a human in the loop. The guarantee is that the operator proposes and the existing gates dispose — the AI gets no path the gates do not already allow — and this almost certainly earns its own ADR when built, because an autonomous driver is a new trust boundary.
- A promoted discovery must be a faithful, replayable witness, not a plausible-looking transcript the model emitted; promotion goes through the same Success Witness and replay as a hand-authored finding, or it is not promoted.
- C2 frameworks are dual-use and high risk tier; their distribution is governed by ADR-0021, and this release is where that governance is exercised, not assumed.
- Open: whether the LLM-application techniques (AI as target) ship here with the AI operator (AI as driver), or split into their own release — settle at /spec.
